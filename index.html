Flatten, project, locate.

A useful implementation view is zᵢ = W · flatten(patchᵢ) + b. A strided 3D convolution can implement the same linear patch projection. Positional encoding gives tokens an address; without position, shuffling a transformer’s inputs would discard their arrangement.

The projection equation is a standard ViT interpretation, not a claim about D4RT’s source code. Figure 7 shows tokenization plus positional encoding without specifying the encoding formula.

Square input, remembered shape.

Before patchification, frames are resized to a square. A separate embedding carries the original width/height ratio. The 6,144 count covers video tokens; the aspect-ratio token is additional.

The selected tubelet has a fixed image address. It does not follow an object as it moves. Learning that correspondence is the encoder’s job.

Does 6,144 tokens mean the video has been compressed into 6,144 numbers?

No. Each token has C continuous coordinates. Token count and storage size are different quantities: feature storage scales with 6,144 × C × bytes per coordinate. These are learned features, not a discrete vocabulary of video codes. The animated-looking bars above are a diagram, not actual D4RT embeddings.

What happens inside a self-attention layer?

For the current token array X, learned projections form Q = XW_Q, K = XW_K and V = XW_V. A token’s query is compared with permitted keys; a softmax converts the scaled dot products into weights over their values. Multiple heads perform different learned comparisons. Their outputs are combined before further feature processing.

The key difference in the decoder is the source of those arrays: Q comes from point-query features, while K and V come from the encoded scene F. The masks above show which comparisons are permitted, not how much weight any comparison receives. These are standard transformer operations; the schematic leaves normalization and residual paths implicit.

Why is full-video attention useful for an occluded point?

Imagine a sleeve disappearing behind a chair and reappearing farther right. A temporal-only edge at the original grid location cannot directly reach the sleeve’s new location. A global layer can. Stacking spatial and temporal layers can also propagate that evidence, but through a different route. Connectivity permits a correspondence; it does not prove that the model learned the correct one.

16 × 16 and 9 × 9 do different jobs.

The 2 × 16 × 16 tubelet tiles the clip for the encoder. The 9 × 9 patch is centered on the requested point in one source frame. Think of a map of the neighborhood plus a close-up of the doorstep. Two nearby queries can share encoder context and still carry different local appearance.

Parallel queries, shared evidence.

The decoder has no query self-attention. Splitting a set of queries into smaller batches therefore preserves their information sources. Cross-attention still depends on N, and the encoder still pays for global self-attention. “Independent” does not mean free.

Score counts use N = 6,144, omitting metadata. M denotes point queries per clip; B in the encoder diagram denotes the clip batch. The width of one attention head is dh.

How can decoding be more detailed than the encoder’s 256 × 256 input?

Queries use continuous normalized image coordinates rather than choosing only encoder patch centers. Appendix C also samples the local RGB patch from original-resolution frames. This allows dense, subpixel queries with local detail even when the shared scene representation was computed at lower resolution. It is not a guarantee of accurate geometry at every requested scale. Derive the displayed gradient

The three target points are (−0.3, 0.2, 2), (0.4, −0.1, 4) and (0.7, 0.3, 6). Their mean depth is 4. Predictions multiply all coordinates by s after adding δ to the third x coordinate, so their mean depth is 4s.

After normalization only one coordinate differs: L = |g((0.7 + δ)/4) − g(0.7/4)| / 3, where g(x) = sign(x) log(1 + |x|). Since g′(x) = 1/(1 + |x|), the derivative away from δ = 0 is sign(δ) / [12(1 + |(0.7 + δ)/4|)]. At zero the demo chooses the zero subgradient.

A fixed gradient step can overshoot the minimum of this absolute-error objective; repeated clicks can oscillate near zero. This is an exercise in differentiating the geometric loss, not a simulation of D4RT’s optimizer or its training convergence.

WHY CAN’T CONFIDENCE JUST COLLAPSE TO ZERO?Isolated objective term
ℓ = c · e − 0.2 log(c)

This exercise holds geometry fixed and varies one confidence term. It is not D4RT’s complete training loss; the slider explores 0 < c ≤ 1 without asserting the head’s activation.

Hard locations get more practice.

30% of sampled queries target depth discontinuities or motion boundaries, using depth-map Sobel responses. Temporal indices are sampled randomly; a 40% branch forces target time to match camera time. The remaining branch can match by chance too.

For 2,048 queries, 30% is approximately 614 boundary-focused examples. They teach the model where smooth interpolation is most likely to fail.

Scale is handled in the objective.

Predicted and target point sets each divide by their own mean depth, then apply sign(x) · log(1 + |x|). Far-away coordinates otherwise dominate an L1 sum. This objective should not be mistaken for direct supervision of absolute metric scale.

Training recipe: data, initialization, augmentation and optimizer

Data. Public and internal sources; named datasets include BlendedMVS, Co3Dv2, Dynamic Replica, Kubric, MVS-Synth, PointOdyssey, ScanNet++, ScanNet, Tartanair, VirtualKitti, and Waymo Open. Ground-truth availability determines the active losses. The paper does not provide a complete reproducible internal-data mixture.

Initialization. The VideoMAE-initialized encoder outperforms training from scratch in the Appendix D ablation. Initialization is separate from D4RT’s supervised, end-to-end geometry training.

Augmentation. Temporally consistent color changes, color drop (0.2), blur (0.4), random crops (scale 0.3–1), aspect-ratio changes, occasional zoom (0.05), and frame-stride sampling. Geometric target coordinates must remain consistent with image transforms.

Optimization. AdamW, weight decay 0.03; 2,500-step warmup to 10⁻⁴, cosine decay to 10⁻⁶, gradient L2 clipping at 10. The authors report just over two days on 64 TPU chips; this is not a workstation runtime estimate.

Exact-loss caveat. Appendix A prints a duplicated confidence coefficient/term. This companion explains the unambiguous confidence-weighted error and log penalty from §2.3 rather than silently inventing a corrected full objective. Appendix A ↗ Appendix D ↗

Why don’t they decode every pixel during training?

One clip contains 48 × 256 × 256 = 3,145,728 possible source pixels, even before choosing target and camera times. Sampling 2,048 queries supplies multiple supervision signals through the same encoder pass. Every sampled query can read the whole scene representation, so sparse output supervision can train shared video features. This does not mean all source locations receive labels on every step.

What is established here, and what is a teaching approximation?

Layer counts, patch sizes, sample counts and loss families are reported specifications. Token arithmetic and attention-score counts are derived here. The moving video, reduced attention grids, confidence plot and model diagram are original teaching tools. They show data flow and mathematical consequences; they do not expose actual trained activations. Exact feature widths, head counts and encoder positional encoding are left unspecified.

A useful distinction

Reconstruction describes what is in a scene. Tracking links the same physical point across time. A sequence of unrelated 3D snapshots does not automatically supply those links.

Read predictions as predictions

A location inferred behind an obstacle is not a new camera observation. This distinction matters whenever an application acts on uncertain geometry.
